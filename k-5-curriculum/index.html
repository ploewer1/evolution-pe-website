Year at a glance

Four quarters, one connected year

Quarter 1 · Weeks 1–9

Movement foundations

Traveling and balance, healthy fuel, rolling and catching, heart and effort, personal movement goals, and a movement carnival.

Quarter 2 · Weeks 10–18

Games and teamwork

Soccer skills, passing and possession, dribbling, shooting, cooperative play, and a sports festival.

Quarter 3 · Weeks 19–27

Rhythm, striking, and rope

Balance sequences, rhythm, hockey-style games, volleying, paddle striking, and jump rope.

Quarter 4 · Weeks 28–36

Strength, habits, and celebration

Strength and endurance, cooperation, digestion and nutrition, fitness progress, outdoor safety, planning, creating a game, and a celebration.

Inside every game

Everything you need on one page

Each game is written to be run, with no guessing about setup, scoring, or what to do when something goes wrong.

  • Objective and an "I can" goal for the child
  • Equipment list with household alternatives
  • Space, setup time, and exact arrangement
  • Numbered play steps, rules, and boundaries
  • Scoring, reset, and round start and end
  • Progressions by grade, plus easier and harder options
  • Safety notes and observable outcomes
  • A health connection that names its source
  • Solo, partner, and group (4 to 20 children) versions
  • A matching Bench Boost topic

Movement is never punishment

Games use skill-based re-entries, like a balance or a short skill, instead of exercises as a penalty. This follows SHAPE America's position against using physical activity as punishment.

Printable library

Game cards, setup diagrams, and recording sheets are designed to print at home or at school.

Health connections with sources

Health ideas name where they come from, such as CDC, NIDDK, and AAP guidance, so you can read further.
